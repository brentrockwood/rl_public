# Rockwood Lab website update

## Project outcome

Update `rockwoodlab.com` so it presents Rockwood Lab as a senior-led applied-AI engineering practice. The site should make one idea immediately clear: Brent Rockwood designs and builds useful, secure, reliable AI systems for real business workflows.

This is a repositioning, not a cosmetic refresh. Remove the former two-person/staffing posture and the broad software/data/embedded menu. Keep the credibility that matters—production engineering, security, senior hands-on delivery, and the ability to take a system from a focused prototype through production.

## Source and audit assumptions

This brief is based on a public-site review of the homepage on 2026-08-13. Confirm every item in the source repository/CMS before shipping; marketing copy and page structure may have changed since the review.

The current homepage appears to contain:

- Hero label: “Applied engineering studio”; headline: “Practical engineering for software, data, and prototypes.”
- Four broad capabilities: full-stack software, data and visualization, embedded/prototypes, and architecture/advisory.
- A staffing/recruiting process headed “Hello to Hired in 2 Weeks or Less,” including developer profiles, candidates, onboarding, account management, and scaling a team.
- A leadership section for Brent Rockwood and Kevin Lim. Kevin is listed as “Lead Developer.”
- A broad contact section covering fractional CTO work, platform modernization, embedded/IoT, data platforms, analytics, and automation.
- Footer language that describes production systems across software, data, and embedded platforms.

The experience/case-study section describes prior work in cybersecurity, Fortune 50 modernization, identity governance, reporting systems, and several linked product sites. These can remain only if they support the AI positioning accurately. Do not imply that a past project used AI unless that is true and approved.

## Non-negotiable changes

1. Remove Kevin Lim’s portrait, name, title, biography, related image assets, alt text, metadata, structured data, and any collaborator/team references.
2. Remove all staff-augmentation/recruiting language and UI: hired-in-two-weeks, candidates, profiles, interviews, team building, onboarding developers, dedicated account manager, and team scaling.
3. Remove or rewrite general-service language that competes with the exclusive AI focus, including embedded/IoT, generic full-stack delivery, generic data/visualization, and fractional CTO positioning.
4. Preserve no claim about AI capability, customers, results, security, privacy, local models, or deployment environment unless it is demonstrably true and approved by Brent.

## Positioning and voice

### Positioning statement

Rockwood Lab builds practical AI systems for teams that need secure, reliable, hands-on engineering.

### Working message

Applied AI engineering for real workflows: internal tools, knowledge systems, agentic automation, evaluation, and the software around them.

### What this is not

- A generic “AI transformation” consultancy.
- A chatbot-reselling agency.
- A recruiting or staff-augmentation firm.
- A promise that AI replaces human judgment or that every workflow should be automated.

### Tone

Clear, technical, grounded, and sparing with hype. Prefer concrete nouns—workflows, systems, evaluation, access controls, data, deployment—over vague claims such as “transform,” “revolutionize,” “unlock,” or “next-generation.” Say what Rockwood Lab will build and how it will be approached. Use first person singular where it reduces ambiguity about who does the work.

## Proposed homepage structure and approved starter copy

Treat the following as the initial copy deck. It may be edited for the existing design system, but retain the meaning and scope.

### 1. Hero

**Eyebrow:** Applied AI engineering

**Headline:** Practical AI systems for real work.

**Body:** Rockwood Lab designs and builds secure, reliable AI systems for teams with important workflows to improve. From a focused prototype to a production deployment, the work stays senior-led and hands-on.

**Primary CTA:** Start a conversation

**Secondary CTA:** See how I work

Do not retain “software, data, and prototypes” as the headline or use “full-stack studio” framing.

### 2. What I build

**Heading:** AI systems that earn a place in the workflow.

**Intro:** The useful work is usually more than a model call. It includes the workflow, the source data, the interface, evaluation, access controls, monitoring, and a path for people to stay in control.

Use three capability cards:

**AI workflow and agent systems**  
Design and build task-focused systems that help teams research, classify, draft, triage, coordinate, and act—with appropriate review and clear boundaries.

**Knowledge and decision tools**  
Turn scattered documents, data, and operating knowledge into useful internal tools with traceability, permissions, and interfaces built for the people doing the work.

**Evaluation, reliability, and secure deployment**  
Make AI systems testable and dependable: define success criteria, evaluate behavior, design failure paths, and deploy with the security and operational controls the context requires.

### 3. How engagements work

**Heading:** Start with one important workflow.

Replace the staffing process with three steps:

1. **Frame the problem** — Identify the workflow, users, constraints, source material, risk, and what success looks like.
2. **Build and test** — Create a focused working system and evaluate it against realistic examples, including failure cases.
3. **Put it to work** — Integrate the system, establish review and operating practices, and improve it using evidence from real use.

No hiring, candidate-selection, team-building, onboarding, account-manager, or scale-the-team language should remain anywhere in this section.

### 4. Credibility / experience

**Heading:** Production experience, applied to AI.

**Body:** Rockwood Lab brings two decades of production engineering and security-minded delivery to AI work. The goal is not a demo that looks clever; it is a system people can use, evaluate, and maintain.

Retain selected prior-work cards only with fact-checked descriptions. Reframe headings toward transferable experience, for example:

- Secure systems and operational intelligence
- Complex platforms and modernization
- Identity, integration, and governance
- High-consequence reporting workflows

Avoid turning this into an unverified AI portfolio. If no direct AI case study is ready, say nothing stronger than the copy above.

### 5. About / leadership

**Heading:** Who you work with

**Name and title:** Brent Rockwood — Founder & Principal Engineer

**Bio:** Brent is a production engineering leader who builds practical AI systems with a focus on security, reliability, and the real constraints of the work. Engagements are led directly by Brent from the first conversation through delivery.

This is a one-person leadership section. Do not leave an empty second card, a two-column team layout that implies a second principal, or stale image files referenced by the build.

### 6. Contact

**Heading:** Have an AI workflow worth improving?

**Body:** Tell me what your team is trying to make easier, faster, safer, or more reliable. A good first conversation covers the people doing the work, the systems and information involved, the constraints, and how you will know the result is useful.

**Contact prompts:**

- What workflow are you trying to improve?
- Who uses it today, and what gets in their way?
- What systems, data, or documents would it need to work with?
- What constraints matter: security, privacy, reliability, deployment, or review?

**Response expectation:** “I respond within one business day.” Keep only if operationally accurate.

### 7. Footer

Replace the broad studio description with:

> Rockwood Lab builds practical AI systems for teams that need dependable, senior-led engineering.

Replace focus tags with AI-specific, accurate terms, such as: `AI workflows`, `Knowledge systems`, `Agent systems`, `Evaluation`, `Secure deployment`.
